I was the first design hire at Cazoo. I hired every researcher and designer who came after me. By the time the organisation reached 65 people, five leaders reported to me: three heads of UX, a head of user research and a head of content design.

That is the headline. The part worth reading is how the standard held while the team grew faster than one person could watch it.

## The situation

Cazoo set out to make buying a used car as easy as buying anything else online. It became the UK's first fully digital, end-to-end car buying experience. The purchase it replaced is stressful, anxious and full of ups and downs.

Design ran across the whole company, for customers and for staff. I worked closely with brand and with logistics and operations. The aim on one side was reassurance for the customer. On the other it was clarity for the people running the business. Both needed the same craft, which is why design sat in one organisation and not two.

Hiring at that speed needs a bar that does not depend on one person.

## One rubric, used twice

The fix was a single rubric. It applied to the people we interviewed and to the people already on the team.

That made hiring and development one continuous process. A candidate was judged against the same skills a designer was grown against, so nobody had to translate between the two.

The rubric sat on two tracks. People could grow by leading people or by leading craft, and neither was a consolation prize for the other.

| Level | Management track | Craft track |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Team lead (squads).** Advanced skill in their craft, emerging skill in leading people. Player-coach for 1 to 3 reports. Shapes growth for one or two practitioners. Counterparts: engineering managers. | **Lead (IC).** Advanced skill in their craft. Shapes how the UX team's skills and tools move forward. Mentors on craft execution. May lead a community of practice. |
| 2 | **Manager (service).** Strategic leadership beyond the UX team. Governs discovery and experience for a part of the business. Full-time cross-team role, 3 to 5 reports. Counterparts: principal engineers and heads of product. | **Principal (service or world).** Strategic leadership of their craft in a service or world. Advances strategy across several teams. Emerging leadership in design advocacy. |
| 3 | **Head of (world or discipline).** Strategic leadership of design across the company. Long-term strategy. Governs discovery, insight and strategy across a world. Counterparts: heads of product. | **Senior principal or architect (world or company).** The same company-wide reach, through craft: long-term strategy for execution and governance of a craft across the company. |

## How it worked, in four moves

1. **Write the bar down.** Specific enough that an interviewer could score against it.
2. **Hire against it.** Every interviewer used the same rubric, inside the team and out.
3. **Grow against it.** People used it to say where they thought they stood, and where a colleague stood.
4. **Let the team argue with it.** The standard was open to challenge, with reasons.

The fourth move did the most work. Because people looked at the rubric continuously, they learned to judge the skills themselves. They talked about those skills and gave each other feedback on where they thought they were. That produced self-reflection and team reflection, and a healthy friction over what the standards should be and why.

One thing the team disagreed about at first was how much user research should matter to senior designers. The rubric ended up with a fork at senior level: management on one path, individual contributor on the other. Before either, every designer had to show they could lead and facilitate research. That made the organisation more research-literate.

## The 2019 targets

The UX objectives for 2019, as written. These were the targets the team set, not results.

| Objective | Key results |
|---|---|
| **Launch a standard-setting online car-buying experience** | NPS above 50 by Boxing Day, with a minimum 20% response rate.<br>Part Exchange and consumer financing at 50 transactions each by Boxing Day.<br>More than 80% of customers who respond report a positive digital purchase experience. |
| **Cultivate a customer-obsessed product culture** | 10 post-sales and 20 potential customers in usability interviews by the end of 2019.<br>Every product team running sustainable, high-quality, self-service user research every two weeks.<br>At least 4 hours of user research exposure per squad member by 1 December. |
| **Make design a differentiator for Cazoo** | More than 85% of customers say the car matched their expectations in follow-up calls.<br>A design system baseline (typography, grids, breakpoints) in production for search and checkout.<br>A design team staffed to meet the organisation's needs: 4 permanent designers, 2 researchers. |
| **Establish a reputation as a product and design leading company** | A permanent designer in every team with consumer-facing UX by December 2019.<br>2 London meetups sponsored before the end of 2019.<br>A design and tech blog, 3 posts, to share how the team works and build the employer brand. |

## What it produced

- **Scale.** From one person to 65, with five leaders reporting to me.
- **Customers.** Over 39,000 five-star Trustpilot reviews, the highest customer satisfaction in UK automotive.
- **Growth.** Revenue up 267% year on year to £174.4m in Q3 2020 to 2021.
- **Markets.** The experience adapted for France, Germany, Spain and Italy, with finance, commercial, legal and marketing.
- **People.** At the time the team was 56% women and 30% non-white, and people were promoted internally more than once. After Cazoo, about seven of the people on my team went on to be directors and leaders, some of them leading design and research organisations at FTSE 100 companies.
- **Ways of working.** Teams were given problems to solve, not features to build, and trusted to solve them their way.

## Where it applies now

I have since moved into product leadership, and the method came with me. It works at any level of maturity: write the standard down, tie hiring to growth, and make the standard something a team is allowed to argue with.

As a product leader fluent in design and research, I've learned to build balanced skills in organisations by shaping clear incentives, structured expectations and creating self-serve development paths for the people I lead, whether in product, design, data or research.
