## The problem

Breaking into film and TV runs on word of mouth and closed networks. Early-career crew have empty credits and a last job behind a bar. Job postings all ask for the same six things, but never tell you which two actually decide it.

Generic AI tools make this worse, not better: they hand you a polished CV you can't defend when someone reads it back to you in an interview.

## The move

Friday takes the opposite position: **it coaches instead of writing**. It scores your CV against how this industry actually hires. How are credits read? Why does crew-list order matter? What is a first job in the camera department really asking for? Then Friday asks you questions until you can tell your own story. That happens to be the exact skill the room tests next.

## Under the hood

Designed, built and shipped in one week with AI-assisted development from start to finish: strategy, design system, LLM-driven coaching flows and the app itself. The model is the easy part. What makes it work is the domain data underneath. Patterns of how productions actually hire are built into the product, so a general model stops averaging every industry at once and starts giving specific advice.

The system remembers. Answers you give once follow you into every future cover letter. Practice interviews know which role you're chasing. Logged outcomes (interview, offer, rejection, silence) feed back into what Friday suggests next. The coaching compounds.

## Why it matters

Friday is in private beta with crew using it to land their first roles. They learn to tell the story of their own work, then walk into rooms able to defend every line on the page. Self-representation is a skill, not a document. And it's the skill that decides who gets a chance.
