I started in **design**. There I learned that every experience is evidence, and the best design decisions are made with data. As a founding hire at Pivotal's Dallas office, I helped companies turn practices into products. At Cazoo I joined at the very beginning. We built the UK's first fully digital car-buying experience, and I stayed through hyper-growth all the way to its $7B IPO. At StepStone I led experience design globally and shipped some of the earliest LLM-powered products in European recruitment.

Then came the crossing into **product**. At Sainsbury's, one of Britain's largest retailers, I led product for enterprise **AI**, ML and data platforms. I took production AI from a handful of experiments to a real platform serving millions of customers. Earlier chapters at Amazon and AT&amp;T taught me how very large organisations actually move.

Somewhere along the way I fell for **data**: not dashboards for their own sake, but the discipline of letting reality vote on our plans. It became the connective tissue between every chapter.

> in·ten·tion·aut *(n.)*: one who navigates by intention. Chooses direction deliberately, before choosing speed.

The word is invented, but the instinct is old. I studied theology before I ever opened a design tool. That was years of training in asking *why* before anyone agrees on *what*. Organising volunteers after Hurricane Sandy taught me something else: organisations behave like products. The way people move through them can be designed. Every roadmap hides a question: why this, why now, why us. Most stalled work traces back to one that never got answered.

I'm open about being trans and neurodivergent. Finding my place took hard work, and it taught me how systems decide who belongs. It turns out thinking hard about who AI and other systems we use daily are built by and for comes naturally when you've been the person they weren't built for. My lived experience has made me a sharper, smarter leader at every level.

My job is getting a team to that answer quickly. Then I keep it honest while it earns its keep in shipped product, experiences customers love, and results the board can see.

I've told these stories on stage too: a keynote at Mind the Product London in 2022, and talks at Turing Fest and Business of Software.

## How I work

- **Translate, don't gatekeep.** I speak designer, engineer, analyst and executive, and I move ideas between them intact.
- **Evidence over ego.** Strong opinions, loosely held, tightly tested.
- **Prototype to decide.** The cheapest way out of an argument is usually a rough version everyone can react to.
- **Software is made of people.** Putting the soft back in software isn't a slogan; it's the operating principle.

Design taught me delight. Data taught me discipline. Product lets me use both.
