I didn't come to product by a straight line — I came in through the studio door.

I started in **design**, where I learned that every pixel is an argument and the best arguments win quietly. From there I crossed into **product**, where those arguments get settled with evidence, trade-offs and trust. Somewhere along the way I fell for **data** — not dashboards for their own sake, but the discipline of letting reality vote on our plans. And now I work where all of it converges: helping teams figure out what **AI** genuinely changes about their product — and what it doesn't.

> in·ten·tion·aut *(n.)* — one who navigates by intention. Chooses direction deliberately, before choosing speed.

That invented word became my compass because it names the thing I kept doing in every role: slowing the room down just long enough to agree on *why* before racing to *what*. Most teams don't lack talent or tooling. They lack a shared answer to the question underneath every roadmap: **why does this deserve to exist?**

My job is getting a team to that answer quickly — and keeping it honest while it earns its keep in shipped product, happy users and numbers that move for the right reasons.

## How I work

- **Translate, don't gatekeep.** I speak designer, engineer, analyst and executive — and I move ideas between them intact.
- **Evidence over ego.** Strong opinions, loosely held, tightly tested.
- **Prototype to decide.** The cheapest way out of an argument is usually a rough version everyone can react to.
- **AI is leverage, not magic.** It amplifies a team's judgment — it doesn't replace it.
