## Patterns across the cohort

### 1. One-line promise naming audience + outcome
Nobody opens with a résumé. Every strong site answers "who is this for and what changes for them" within the first screen.

- Lenny Rachitsky: "Deeply researched product, growth, and career advice for product leaders, founders, and ambitious builders."
- Janna Bastow: "I help product leaders get unstuck: through coaching, consulting, and 20 years of learning the hard way."
- Ken Norton: eyebrow "Executive coaching for product leaders"; headline "Stop tweaking and start transforming."

### 2. Quantified proof placed beside the ask
Credibility sits directly next to offers, never on a separate page. It is always specific and verifiable.

- Gibson Biddle publishes his talk NPS (69, n=49) and links the raw survey.
- Melissa Perri: "Escaping the Build Trap has sold over 75,000 copies"; named clients include Capital One, Vanguard, Walmart/Sam's Club.
- Martin Eriksson: "advising 150+ companies" plus a Marty Cagan testimonial.
- Norton: testimonials with names, titles and companies (Asana SVP, Flatiron Health CPO).

### 3. Free content is the front door; services are the checkout
All reachable sites lead with freely consumable value (essays, guides, podcasts) and monetize one step downstream (courses, workshops, coaching, books). Even pure link-hubs work when the writing carries the reputation (Zhuo).

### 4. Owned audience
Email capture is the hardest CTA almost everywhere: Lenny, Mironov, Rekhi, Pichler, Eyal (160k+ subscribers). Independent leaders treat the list as the asset; Substack-only presence is rare.

### 5. A named idea they own
Proprietary frameworks make leaders findable and quotable:

- Petra Wille: STRONG (book, cards, course, community)
- Nir Eyal: Hooked / Behavioral Design
- Teresa Torres: Opportunity Solution Trees, Product Trios
- Martin Eriksson: The Decision Stack

### 6. Diagnosis before service
Norton mirrors reader pain verbatim ("Do any of these struggles sound familiar?") before describing his offer. Services are framed as answers to named struggles.

## What works on intentionaut.com

1. Voice and hook. "I speak four languages: design, product, data & AI" compresses identity into one memorable line; same spirit as Zhuo's parallel role list. The manifesto line ("Most teams don't lack talent or tools. They lack intention.") is philosophy-first, matching Wille and Liu.
2. Restraint. Minimal type-led design puts us in the top-quartile aesthetic camp (Zhuo, Eriksson), not the busy logo-wall camp.
3. Services-as-nav. Three lanes (leadership & advisory, speaking, writing), each one click from the hero. Matches Eriksson's pattern.
4. Published rates. Almost none of the 20 show prices. Ours filters poor-fit enquiries, signals respect, and the sliding-scale honesty builds trust.

## Gaps, ranked

1. No proof anywhere. Zero numbers, testimonials or client references on homepage or contact. Every leader site shows evidence adjacent to its ask. Biggest gap by far.
2. Positioning is about craft, not customer. The hero answers "who is Saielle?" but not "what changes for me, and who is me?" The audience (founders? scale-ups? product leaders?) is never named.
3. No audience capture. Writing routes to Substack with no email ask of our own. The largest mechanical gap versus the cohort.
4. No owned framework. "Intention" is unclaimed IP sitting in our domain name and manifesto. It could be coined into a named method that essays compound around, like STRONG or Decision Stack.
5. Lanes describe services abstractly. No pain-mirroring before the offer.
6. Placeholder social proof on Speaking (sample talk still live) and no og-image yet.

## Recommended next moves

Priority order:

1. Collect 2 to 3 testimonials with titles (even anonymized, e.g. "CPO, UK scale-up"). Place beside the rates table and surface one line of proof on the homepage.
2. Rewrite the hero lede to name audience + outcome in one sentence. Keep the four-languages line as flavor, not load-bearing structure.
3. Add email capture: a prominent "get new essays" block wired to the Substack embed or a lightweight form.
4. Coin and name the framework (candidate territory: deliberate direction, intentional products). Give it away in essays; attach services to it later.
5. Replace the sample talk with real talks via CMS; add an og-image (public/og.png).
