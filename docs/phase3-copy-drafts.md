## 3. Service page expansions

The existing intro / "What I do" / "How it works" copy on each page stays.
These are the sections to add, to get each page to ~700–900 words with a real
FAQ (which also gets FAQPage schema).

### 3a. `/fractional/` — additions

**Who this is for**
- A founder or exec team carrying product direction on top of everything else
- A team with the talent and the tools but no senior product voice in the room
- A board that wants a roadmap when what is missing is a thesis
- A permanent CPO search that will take six months you do not have

**Who this is not for**
- Teams that want a deck and a consultant who leaves. I own outcomes, or I pass.
- A quick rescue opinion. That is what a strategy session is for.
- Anyone wanting a name on an org chart rather than someone in the work.

**What tends to change**
Within the first month: a shared answer to "what are we building and why", and
one or two decisions that were stuck get made. By the handover: an operating
model that runs without me, a team that has built the muscle, and a permanent
hire brief that describes the real job.

**FAQ** (drives schema)
- *How many days a week?* One or two, on an agreed rhythm, three months minimum.
- *Interim or fractional?* Fractional is part-time and ongoing. Interim is
  closer to full-time and covers a seat while you run a search. I do both; we
  decide which on the first call.
- *Do you replace our need for a permanent CPO?* No. I make the case for one
  clearer, and often help you hire them.
- *What does it cost?* [rate] a day. Sliding scale for bootstrapped and
  nonprofit teams — ask.

### 3b. `/strategic-direction/` — additions

**Who this is for**
- A leadership team with three strategies and no shared one
- An opportunity that is real but has not been framed as a problem yet
- A board asking for a roadmap before there is a thesis

**Who this is not for**
- Teams that already know the problem and need help executing. That is the
  operating model work.
- Anyone looking for a strategy deck to present rather than a decision to make.

**What you leave with**
A direction document your team can act on immediately: the problem framed, the
thesis the leadership team actually believes, the prioritisation logic, and the
measures that tell you whether you are making progress. Not a report.

**FAQ**
- *How long?* Two to four weeks, two or three days a week.
- *Who is in the room?* Your leadership team. This is not something I do to you.
- *Is this a roadmap?* No. A roadmap is an output of direction, not a substitute
  for it.
- *What if we disagree with where it lands?* Then we have surfaced a real
  disagreement early, which is the point.

### 3c. `/execution/` — additions

**Who this is for**
- A team that has bought the technology and the strategy deck but not built the
  product and operational system around it
- Organisations that acquired AI or data capability with no product thinking
  attached
- Anywhere three reorgs in two years have not fixed the thing

**Who this is not for**
- Teams still arguing about what to build. That is the strategy work first.
- Anyone who wants an org chart redrawn rather than a system that runs.

**What tends to change**
Portfolio decisions stop being relitigated. Teams can explain what the strategy
means for their Monday. Governance moves from a bottleneck to an enabler. And
there is a rhythm that keeps strategy connected to delivery after I step back.

**FAQ**
- *How long?* Four to twelve weeks, two or three days a week.
- *Is this a reorg?* Sometimes structure changes, but structure is the last
  lever, not the first.
- *Does this work for AI and data teams specifically?* Yes; that is most of
  what I do now. The gap is usually product thinking, not technology.
- *What do we keep afterwards?* An operating model your team runs
  independently, and the governance rhythms that hold it together.

- *What does it cost?* From [rate] for an event. Workshops quoted by scope.
  Sliding scale applies.
- *Can you tailor it to our company?* That is the point of the private format.
